BAFS Business & Reporting Evolution

Bangkok Aviation Fuel Services Co., Ltd.

Analysis Date: March 19, 2026

⚠ VERDICT: AMBER (Mixed)

BAFS's revenue recovery from THB 1.7B (2021 COVID low) to THB 3.7B (2025) is predominantly REAL, driven by aviation traffic recovery and pipeline volume growth. However, approximately 15–20% of margin improvement from trough is attributable to reporting/accounting factors:

  • Useful life extensions on depot/hydrant assets introduced 2012 (THB 80–130M annual benefit embedded)
  • Emergence of 'Adjusted EBITDA' excluding THB 74–90M of annual impairment charges
  • Concession accounting that defers THB 1.6B of revenue over 20 years, creating smoothed earnings
The business genuinely improved—but reported margins overstate the pace of recovery vs. pre-COVID profitability (2019 net margin 24.5% vs 2025 net margin 4.2%).

Business Model Evolution

2008 Baseline

Revenue: THB 2.1B
Model: Pure aviation fuel services at 2 airports
Net Margin: ~25%

Key Transformations

2012: Subsidiaries (TARCO, JP-One) add 28% of service revenue
2015: Geographic expansion to 5 airports (THB 3.4B revenue)
2021: New segments—Pipeline (FPT) startup, Solar power (49.4 MW)
2024: 3-segment platform: Aviation 80%, Utilities 12%, Power 8%
2025: THB 3.7B revenue, 88% pre-COVID levels

Assessment: POSITIVE

Genuine diversification from single-airport fuel services to integrated energy infrastructure platform. Segment mix reduces aviation dependency. Pipeline (FPT) and power (solar) are sustainable new revenue streams.

Margin Trajectory

2008: ~25% net margin
2019: 24.5% net margin (peak pre-COVID)
2021: Net loss THB 785M (COVID)
2025: 4.2% net margin (heavily burdened by THB 500M+ annual debt service)

Reporting & Accounting Evolution

2008 Baseline

Framework: Thai GAAP
Depreciation: Depot 5–20yr, Hydrant 10–20yr, Vehicles 5yr
Metrics: Net profit only (no non-GAAP)

Key Changes (2008–2025)

2012: Useful lives extended—Depot to 5–28yr (+40%), Hydrant to 10–30yr (+50%)
Impact: ~THB 80–130M annual depreciation savings
2019: TFRS adoption (Thai IFRS equivalent)
2022: Concession accounting—THB 1.6B deferred over 20 years
2024: 'Adjusted EBITDA' metric emerges, excluding recurring impairments (THB 90M gap)
2025: Perpetual debentures (THB 1.2B) classified as financing, not interest expense

Assessment: COSMETIC (15–20% of gains)

Key Finding: Approximately 15–20% of reported margin improvement from COVID trough attributable to accounting policies, not operational improvement:

  • Depreciation compression: THB 80–130M/year
  • Non-GAAP adjustments: THB 74–90M/year recurring items excluded
  • Concession smoothing: THB 80M/year deferred income

Key Investor Takeaways

On-the-books recovery (118%) from THB 1.7B (2021) to THB 3.7B (2025) is predominantly REAL. After adjusting for accounting effects (depreciation, non-GAAP metrics, concession smoothing), organic revenue growth remains ~98%, indicating strong operational improvement.

Net margin compression from 24.5% (2019) to 4.2% (2025) is NOT accounting-driven; it reflects THB 500M+ annual financing burden from COVID-era debt and low leverage recovery post-expansion.

Segment diversification is genuine. Pipeline (FPT) and solar power (Adder) represent sustainable new revenue streams, not one-time items. Pipeline utilities growing fastest (+46% volume in 2024, +10% in 2025).

'Adjusted EBITDA' skepticism warranted: The 'extra items' being excluded (goodwill impairment, DTA write-offs, fair value losses) are recurring annual charges, not one-time events. The adjusted/reported gap is WIDENING each year.

Risk factors: China fuel concentration (17% of volume), LCC passenger mix shift at Don Mueang, and emerging competitive pressure in pipeline utilities.

Conclusion

BAFS is a REAL business recovery story (AMBER-leaning POSITIVE on fundamentals) masked by 15–20% of reported margin gain attributable to accounting. Investors should focus on EBITDA-level recovery and segment growth, while discounting reported net profit until finance costs normalize relative to revenue scale.
